# Sources & literature map — Study 994 (The Rounding Tax)

## Indivisibility as a portfolio constraint

- **Mansini, R. & Speranza, M. G. (1999), "Heuristic Algorithms for the Portfolio Selection
  Problem with Minimum Transaction Lots", *European Journal of Operational Research* 114(2),
  219-233.** The formal treatment of lot constraints in portfolio optimisation, and why the
  problem becomes combinatorial rather than convex.
- **Kellerer, H., Mansini, R. & Speranza, M. G. (2000), "Selecting Portfolios with Fixed Costs
  and Minimum Transaction Lots", *Annals of Operations Research* 99, 287-304.** Fixed costs and
  minimum lots together — the small-account problem stated properly.
- **Balbás, A., Mayoral, S. & Ortobelli, S. (2005), "Trade-off Between Diversification and
  Indivisibility".** Directly relevant to this study's finding that fewer funds beats more.

## Largest-remainder allocation

- **Balinski, M. L. & Young, H. P. (2001), *Fair Representation: Meeting the Ideal of One Man,
  One Vote*, 2nd ed., Brookings.** The apportionment problem — mathematically identical to
  turning target weights into whole share counts, and the source of the largest-remainder method
  used here rather than naive flooring.

## Rebalancing with frictions

- **Donohue, C. & Yip, K. (2003), "Optimal Portfolio Rebalancing with Transaction Costs",
  *Journal of Portfolio Management* 29(4), 49-63.** No-trade bands, and why they widen as costs
  rise — the mechanism behind section 8.
- **Masters, S. J. (2003), "Rebalancing", *Journal of Portfolio Management* 29(3), 52-57.** The
  practitioner treatment of tolerance bands.

## Small accounts specifically

- **Barber, B. M. & Odean, T. (2000), "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth", *Journal of Finance*
  55(2), 773-806.** Small accounts, high turnover, and the costs that follow — the broader
  context in which this particular friction sits.
- **Investment Company Institute, *Investment Company Fact Book* (annual).** Account-size
  distributions, which set how many real investors are inside the range where this matters.
