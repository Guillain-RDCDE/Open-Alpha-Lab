# References & literature map — Study 547 (Blue-Monday)

## The claim, at its (non-)source

- **Cliff Arnall (2005)**, the 'Blue Monday' formula. A pseudo-scientific 'equation' naming the
  third Monday of January the most depressing day of the year, commissioned as a **PR stunt for
  Sky Travel**. It has no peer-reviewed basis and has been repeatedly criticised as junk science
  (e.g. Dean Burnett, *The Guardian*, and Ben Goldacre's *Bad Science* columns). The market
  question this study asks: even if the mood claim were true, does it show up on the tape?
- **Popular debunkings** — psychologists and science communicators note the 'equation' mixes
  incommensurable units (weather, debt, days since Christmas, motivation) with arbitrary weights
  and cannot be derived or falsified. Blue Monday is folklore, not a finding.

## The mood-anomaly literature it borrows from

- **Kamstra, Kramer & Levi (2003)**, *"Winter Blues: A SAD Stock Market Cycle."* *American
  Economic Review* 93(1). The serious cousin: Seasonal Affective Disorder is argued to raise
  investor risk aversion in autumn/winter, depressing then reviving equity returns. This desk
  tested it directly in **[Study 150 — SAD-Effect](../../150-sad-effect/)** and found it a
  data-mining artifact. Blue Monday is a single-day, calendar-pinned special case of the same
  mood-drives-markets thesis.
- **Hirshleifer & Shumway (2003)**, *"Good Day Sunshine: Stock Returns and the Weather."* *Journal
  of Finance* 58(3). Morning sunshine correlates with (small) positive returns — the broader
  'weather/mood affects markets' program Blue Monday sits inside.
- **Saunders (1993)**, *"Stock Prices and Wall Street Weather."* *American Economic Review* 83(5).
  The early mood-and-markets result (NYC cloud cover vs same-day returns).

## Calendar-anomaly method neighbours on this bench

- **[Study 150 — SAD-Effect](../../150-sad-effect/)** — the seasonal-mood parent. Blue Monday is
  its one-day January special case; both fail the inference bar.
- **[Study 224 — Monday-Effect](../../224-monday-effect/)** — the day-of-week (weekend) effect.
  Blue Monday is a *Monday* claim; Study 224 shows Mondays are not negative on the SPY tape at all,
  which already undercuts a 'worst Monday' story.
- **[Study 223 — Same-Month-Seasonality](../../223-same-month-seasonality/)** /
  **[Study 89 — Turn-of-the-Month](../../89-turn-of-the-month/)** — sibling calendar seasonals; the
  same random-relabelling placebo logic applies (any date can be data-mined into a 'pattern').

## Shared method

- **Welch (1947)** — the unequal-variance two-sample *t* for the Blue vs baseline-day return
  difference.
- **Label-shuffle / permutation testing** (Fisher 1935; Good 2005) — the calendar-aware placebo:
  relabel a random Monday (or random trading day) as pseudo-Blue and read the tail probability.
- The **MLK-holiday collision** — the Uniform Monday Holiday Act (1968, effective 1971) fixed
  several US federal holidays to Mondays; Martin Luther King Jr. Day (federal since 1986) falls on
  the *third Monday of January*, exactly Blue Monday, and NYSE/Nasdaq have closed for it since 1998.
  This is why the literal claim is untestable on a real equity tape.
- House methodology: [`METHODOLOGY.md`](../../../METHODOLOGY.md) — the inference bar (a two-sample
  *t* ≥ 2 plus a placebo null and seed-robustness), gross-and-net labelling, shorts pay borrow, and
  the thin-sample caveat named on the SIGNAL axis.
