# Sources & literature map — Study 1003 (The 1% Allocation)

## Why optimisers produce confident nonsense

- **Michaud, R. O. (1989), "The Markowitz Optimization Enigma: Is 'Optimized' Optimal?",
  *Financial Analysts Journal* 45(1), 31-42.** Mean-variance optimisers as error maximisers —
  the framing this study applies to the highest-volatility case available.
- **Best, M. J. & Grauer, R. R. (1991), "On the Sensitivity of Mean-Variance-Efficient Portfolios
  to Changes in Asset Means", *Review of Financial Studies* 4(2), 315-342.** The analytic
  sensitivity result behind the bootstrap in section 4.
- **Merton, R. C. (1980), "On Estimating the Expected Return on the Market", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 8(4), 323-361.** The foundational statement that expected returns need
  enormously long samples while variances do not — the arithmetic in section 6 is Merton's.
- **DeMiguel, V., Garlappi, L. & Uppal, R. (2009), "Optimal Versus Naive Diversification",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 22(5), 1915-1953.** Estimation error large enough that 1/N beats
  the optimiser out of sample.
- **Jobson, J. D. & Korkie, B. (1981), "Performance Hypothesis Testing with the Sharpe and
  Treynor Measures", *Journal of Finance* 36(4), 889-908.** The distribution theory behind
  asking whether two Sharpe ratios differ, which is what section 5 does by bootstrap.

## Bitcoin as a portfolio asset

- **Yermack, D. (2015), "Is Bitcoin a Real Currency? An Economic Appraisal", in *Handbook of
  Digital Currency*, Elsevier, 31-43.**
- **Liu, Y. & Tsyvinski, A. (2021), "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency", *Review of Financial
  Studies* 34(6), 2689-2727.** Finds crypto returns unexplained by conventional factors, with
  standard errors that make the point of this study for it.
- **Bianchi, D. (2020), "Cryptocurrencies as an Asset Class? An Empirical Assessment", *Journal
  of Alternative Investments* 23(2), 162-179.**
- **Baur, D. G., Hong, K. & Lee, A. D. (2018), "Bitcoin: Medium of Exchange or Speculative
  Assets?", *Journal of International Financial Markets, Institutions and Money* 54, 177-189.**

## Method

- **Politis, D. N. & Romano, J. P. (1994), "The Stationary Bootstrap", *Journal of the American
  Statistical Association* 89(428), 1303-1313.** The block resampling used in sections 4 and 5.
