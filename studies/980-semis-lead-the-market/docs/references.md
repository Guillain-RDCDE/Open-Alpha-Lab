# Sources & literature map — Study 980 (The Silicon Canary)

## Cross-asset and cross-sector lead-lag

- **Lo, A. W. & MacKinlay, A. C. (1990), "When Are Contrarian Profits Due to Stock Market
  Overreaction?", *Review of Financial Studies* 3(2), 175-205.** The founding treatment of
  lead-lag between portfolios, and of how much of it is non-synchronous trading rather than
  information.
- **Hou, K. (2007), "Industry Information Diffusion and the Lead-Lag Effect in Stock Returns",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 20(4), 1113-1138.** Information diffuses *within* industries
  first; the cleanest evidence that lead-lag effects are real but small and mostly intra-sector.
- **Menzly, L. & Ozbas, O. (2010), "Market Segmentation and Cross-Predictability of Returns",
  *Journal of Finance* 65(4), 1555-1580.** Economically linked industries predict one another —
  the supply-chain mechanism the semiconductor claim assumes.
- **Cohen, L. & Frazzini, A. (2008), "Economic Links and Predictable Returns", *Journal of
  Finance* 63(4), 1977-2011.** Customer-supplier links predict returns; the strongest published
  version of the mechanism, and it works at the *firm* level, not the sector-index level.

## Semiconductors specifically

- **Semiconductor Industry Association, monthly billings data**, and the book-to-bill ratio
  that practitioners actually mean when they say semis lead. This study tests the *price*
  version of the claim, which is the weaker one.
- **Chen, N.-F., Roll, R. & Ross, S. A. (1986), "Economic Forces and the Stock Market",
  *Journal of Business* 59(3), 383-403.** Why a cyclical sector's returns can look like a
  macro leading indicator without carrying any information the market does not have.

## Getting the test right

- **Hansen, L. P. & Hodrick, R. J. (1980), *Journal of Political Economy* 88(5), 829-853**, and
  **Newey & West (1987), *Econometrica* 55(3), 703-708.** The overlapping-window standard errors
  used in every regression here.
- **Granger, C. W. J. (1969), "Investigating Causal Relations by Econometric Models and
  Cross-Spectral Methods", *Econometrica* 37(3), 424-438.** What "leads" means formally, and why
  the reverse direction must always be reported.
- **Sullivan, R., Timmermann, A. & White, H. (1999), "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule
  Performance, and the Bootstrap", *Journal of Finance* 54(5), 1647-1691.** Why a grid of
  lookbacks and horizons needs its multiplicity counted, which this study does explicitly.
