# Sources & literature map — Study 984 (A Dollar Off)

## The original claim and the tax reading

- **Campbell, J. A. & Beranek, W. (1955), "Stock Price Behavior on Ex-Dividend Dates", *Journal
  of Finance* 10(4), 425-429.** The first measurement: the price falls by less than the
  dividend.
- **Elton, E. J. & Gruber, M. J. (1970), "Marginal Stockholder Tax Rates and the Clientele
  Effect", *Review of Economics and Statistics* 52(1), 68-74.** The 0.778 that this study's
  bootstrap interval is compared against, and the tax-clientele interpretation.
- **Kalay, A. (1982), "The Ex-Dividend Day Behavior of Stock Prices: A Re-Examination of the
  Clientele Effect", *Journal of Finance* 37(4), 1059-1070.** The reply: short-term traders,
  not tax clienteles, and transaction costs set the bounds — which is exactly the tension the
  capture-trade section prices.

## Why the measurement is the hard part

- **Boyd, J. H. & Jagannathan, R. (1994), "Ex-Dividend Price Behavior of Common Stocks", *Review
  of Financial Studies* 7(4), 711-741.** The market adjustment and the non-linearity in yield;
  the methodological ancestor of section 3 here.
- **Bali, R. & Hite, G. L. (1998), "Ex-Dividend Day Stock Price Behavior: Discreteness or
  Tax-Induced Clienteles?", *Journal of Financial Economics* 47(2), 127-159.** Price
  discreteness as the explanation — the reason this study starts after decimalisation and says
  so.
- **Frank, M. & Jagannathan, R. (1998), "Why Do Stock Prices Drop by Less Than the Value of the
  Dividend?", *Journal of Financial Economics* 47(2), 161-188.** A market with no dividend taxes
  at all that still shows the effect — evidence that microstructure, not tax, may be doing the
  work.
- **Graham, J. R., Michaely, R. & Roberts, M. R. (2003), "Do Price Discreteness and Transactions
  Costs Affect Stock Returns?", *Journal of Finance* 58(6), 2611-2635.** The decimalisation
  natural experiment.

## The statistics of a ratio with a small denominator

- **Fieller, E. C. (1954), "Some Problems in Interval Estimation", *JRSS B* 16(2), 175-185.**
  Why a ratio estimator's confidence interval is not a normal interval around a point estimate.
- **Marsaglia, G. (2006), "Ratios of Normal Variables", *Journal of Statistical Software* 16(4).**
  The distribution of a ratio whose denominator can be near zero — the reason a third of the
  events here score outside [0, 2].
