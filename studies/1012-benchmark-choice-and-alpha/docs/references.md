# Sources & literature map — Study 1012 (Choose Your Benchmark)

## Benchmark specification and alpha

- **Roll, R. (1978), "Ambiguity When Performance Is Measured by the Securities Market Line",
  *Journal of Finance* 33(4), 1051-1069.** Roll's critique: performance rankings depend on the
  benchmark, and the benchmark is unobservable. This study is Roll's point, measured.
- **Lehmann, B. N. & Modest, D. M. (1987), "Mutual Fund Performance Evaluation: A Comparison of
  Benchmarks and Benchmark Comparisons", *Journal of Finance* 42(2), 233-265.** The direct
  antecedent of section 1's grid.
- **Grinblatt, M. & Titman, S. (1994), "A Study of Monthly Mutual Fund Returns and Performance
  Evaluation Techniques", *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 29(3), 419-444.**
- **Chan, L. K. C., Dimmock, S. G. & Lakonishok, J. (2009), "Benchmarking Money Manager
  Performance: Issues and Evidence", *Review of Financial Studies* 22(11), 4553-4599.** Finds
  benchmark choice moves measured performance by amounts comparable to the alphas under debate.
- **Sensoy, B. A. (2009), "Performance Evaluation and Self-Designated Benchmark Indexes in the
  Mutual Fund Industry", *Journal of Financial Economics* 92(1), 25-39.** Funds choose
  benchmarks that flatter them, and investors respond to the flattered number — the empirical
  counterpart to section 4.

## Factor models

- **Fama, E. F. & French, K. R. (1993), "Common Risk Factors in the Returns on Stocks and
  Bonds", *Journal of Financial Economics* 33(1), 3-56.**
- **Carhart, M. M. (1997), "On Persistence in Mutual Fund Performance", *Journal of Finance*
  52(1), 57-82.**
- **Fama, E. F. & French, K. R. (2015), "A Five-Factor Asset Pricing Model", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 116(1), 1-22.** Each addition shrinks alphas that the previous model
  called skill — the ladder in section 2, in the literature's own history.
- **Harvey, C. R., Liu, Y. & Zhu, H. (2016), "… and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 5-68.** On the multiplicity problem that makes "which
  factors" undecidable in practice.

## Method

- **Newey, W. K. & West, K. D. (1987), "A Simple, Positive Semi-Definite, Heteroskedasticity and
  Autocorrelation Consistent Covariance Matrix", *Econometrica* 55(3), 703-708.**
- **Fama, E. F. (1998), "Market Efficiency, Long-Term Returns, and Behavioral Finance",
  *Journal of Financial Economics* 49(3), 283-306.** On how sensitive long-horizon anomalies are
  to the model of expected returns — the same argument in a different setting.
