# Literature map — Study 144 (Permanent-Portfolio)

## Original source

1. **Browne, H. (1987).** *Why the Best-Laid Investment Plans Usually Go Wrong.*
   Harper & Row. The original book-length case for the Permanent Portfolio: four
   equal parts, one for each macroeconomic regime (prosperity, inflation, deflation,
   recession), rebalanced when any leg drifts beyond 15-35% of portfolio.

2. **Browne, H. (1999).** *Fail-Safe Investing: Lifelong Financial Security in 30
   Minutes.* St. Martin's Griffin. The distilled version of the PP recipe. The
   canonical reference: 25% stocks, 25% gold, 25% long Treasuries, 25% T-bills,
   rebalance annually or when a leg reaches 35%/15% band.

## Academic treatment

3. **Faber, M. (2013).** "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation."
   *Journal of Wealth Management*, updated 2013. Includes backtests of the Permanent
   Portfolio alongside other static and dynamic allocation frameworks. Available on
   SSRN: 962461.

4. **Antonacci, G. (2014).** *Dual Momentum Investing.* McGraw-Hill. Chapter 4
   benchmarks the PP against momentum-based allocation; finds the PP's low volatility
   comes at a meaningful CAGR cost relative to trend-following alternatives.

5. **Bernstein, W. (2002).** *The Four Pillars of Investing.* McGraw-Hill. Chapter 10
   discusses the theory of permanent diversification across asset classes; provides
   a theoretical underpinning for regime-based allocation philosophy.

6. **Ilmanen, A. (2011).** *Expected Returns.* Wiley. Chapter 16, "Multi-Asset
   Strategies," analyses the conditions under which defensive blends (gold + bonds)
   provide genuine diversification versus spurious correlation from a shared
   disinflation regime tailwind. Directly relevant to the window-caveat in Study 144.

7. **Asness, C., Frazzini, A., & Pedersen, L. (2012).** "Leverage Aversion and Risk
   Parity." *Financial Analysts Journal* 68(1). Shows that the Sharpe-improvement
   from diversified blends (risk parity being the vol-weighted generalisation of the
   PP) is real but requires leverage to match equity returns — exactly the PP's
   trade-off. Compares to Study 68 (All-Weather) in this repo.

8. **Dalio, R. (2004).** Bridgewater Associates white paper, "Engineering Targeted
   Returns and Risks." The "All Seasons" framework (risk-parity) is the institutional
   cousin of the PP; Dalio's regime classification (growth up/down, inflation up/down)
   mirrors Browne's four-regime logic. Contrasted in Study 68 (this repo).

## Empirical comparisons

9. **Portfolio Visualizer (2024).** "Backtest Portfolio Asset Allocation — Permanent
   Portfolio." https://www.portfoliovisualizer.com. Interactive historical back-test
   from 1978 (using proxies pre-GLD); shows the 1970s inflation period where gold
   was the single largest contributor; and the 2013/2019 period where the PP trailed
   SPY by 20+ pp in a single year.

10. **Meb Faber Research Blog (2013).** "The Permanent Portfolio, Revisited."
    https://mebfaber.com. Replicates Browne's results with ETF proxies; highlights
    the recency and regime-sensitivity of the historical Sharpe advantage.

## The 2022 stress test

11. **Klement, J. (2022).** "The 60/40 Portfolio is Dead — What Should Replace It?"
    *Klement on Investing* newsletter (Substack). Analyses the 2022 joint bond-equity
    drawdown and its implications for diversified fixed-weight portfolios including the
    PP. Directly referenced in the 2022 row of the results table.
