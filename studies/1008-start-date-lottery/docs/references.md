# Sources & literature map — Study 1008 (The Start-Date Lottery)

## Sequence risk

- **Milevsky, M. A. & Robinson, C. (2005), "A Sustainable Spending Rate without Simulation",
  *Financial Analysts Journal* 61(6), 89-100.** Analytic treatment of ruin probability under
  withdrawals — the framework behind section 4.
- **Bengen, W. P. (1994), "Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data", *Journal of
  Financial Planning* 7(4), 171-180.** The origin of the 4% rule, and of start-date analysis in
  retirement planning.
- **Cooley, P. L., Hubbard, C. M. & Walz, D. T. (1998), "Retirement Savings: Choosing a
  Withdrawal Rate That Is Sustainable", *AAII Journal* 20(2), 16-21.** The Trinity study.
- **Kitces, M. & Pfau, W. D. (2015), "Retirement Risk, Rising Equity Glide Paths, and Valuation-
  Based Asset Allocation", *Journal of Financial Planning* 28(3).** Argues for *rising* equity
  in retirement — the opposite of convention, and consistent with section 3's profile.

## Glide paths and lifecycle allocation

- **Bodie, Z., Merton, R. C. & Samuelson, W. F. (1992), "Labor Supply Flexibility and Portfolio
  Choice in a Life Cycle Model", *Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control* 16(3-4), 427-449.**
  Why human capital, not horizon per se, justifies a glide path.
- **Poterba, J., Rauh, J., Venti, S. & Wise, D. (2007), "Defined Contribution Plans, Defined
  Benefit Plans, and the Accumulation of Retirement Wealth", *Journal of Public Economics*
  91(10), 2062-2086.**
- **Estrada, J. (2014), "The Glidepath Illusion: An International Perspective", *Journal of
  Portfolio Management* 40(4), 52-64.** Finds conventional declining glide paths dominated in
  19 countries — the direct antecedent of section 5's two-column scoring.
- **Arnott, R. D., Sherrerd, K. F. & Wu, L. (2013), "The Glidepath Illusion... and Potential
  Solutions", *Journal of Retirement* 1(2), 13-28.**

## Dollar-cost averaging

- **Constantinides, G. M. (1979), "A Note on the Suboptimality of Dollar-Cost Averaging as an
  Investment Policy", *Journal of Financial and Economic Research* 4, 443-450.** Averaging in is
  suboptimal in expectation — which is consistent with section 6, where it reduces *dispersion*
  rather than raising the mean.
- **Rozeff, M. S. (1994), "Lump-Sum Investing versus Dollar-Averaging", *Journal of Portfolio
  Management* 20(2), 45-50.**
