# Sources & literature map — Study 993 (Down Hurts More)

## The original claim

- **Black, F. (1976), "Studies of Stock Price Volatility Changes", *Proceedings of the 1976
  Meetings of the American Statistical Association*, 177-181.** The observation and the leverage
  explanation, in three pages.
- **Christie, A. A. (1982), "The Stochastic Behavior of Common Stock Variances", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 10(4), 407-432.** The first careful test of Black's mechanism, and the
  first finding that the magnitudes do not fit.

## Why the name is probably wrong

- **Figlewski, S. & Wang, X. (2000), "Is the 'Leverage Effect' a Leverage Effect?", SSRN
  256109.** The decisive paper: the effect appears in firms with no debt, does not appear when
  leverage changes for reasons other than price, and is far too large for realistic debt levels.
  This study's gold and Bitcoin columns are the same argument made with assets instead of firms.
- **Hasanhodzic, J. & Lo, A. W. (2019), "On Black's Leverage Effect in Firms with No Leverage",
  *Journal of Portfolio Management* 46(1), 106-122.** All-equity firms show the same asymmetry.
- **Bouchaud, J.-P., Matacz, A. & Potters, M. (2001), "Leverage Effect in Financial Markets: The
  Retarded Volatility Model", *Physical Review Letters* 87(22), 228701.** A quantitative
  characterisation of the decay, and evidence that the effect differs between indices and
  individual stocks in a way leverage cannot explain.

## The competing mechanism

- **Campbell, J. Y. & Hentschel, L. (1992), "No News Is Good News: An Asymmetric Model of
  Changing Volatility in Stock Returns", *Journal of Financial Economics* 31(3), 281-318.** The
  volatility-feedback story: causality runs from volatility to price, not the other way.
- **Bekaert, G. & Wu, G. (2000), "Asymmetric Volatility and Risk in Equity Markets", *Review of
  Financial Studies* 13(1), 1-42.** Tests both mechanisms jointly and finds feedback dominant.
- **Bollerslev, T., Litvinova, J. & Tauchen, G. (2006), "Leverage and Volatility Feedback Effects
  in High-Frequency Data", *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 4(3), 353-384.** The lead-lag
  design this study's section 7 imitates, run at high frequency where the proxy noise is far
  smaller.

## Measuring it

- **Nelson, D. B. (1991), "Conditional Heteroskedasticity in Asset Returns: A New Approach",
  *Econometrica* 59(2), 347-370.** EGARCH, and the gamma parameter fitted here.
- **Glosten, L. R., Jagannathan, R. & Runkle, D. E. (1993), *Journal of Finance* 48(5),
  1779-1801.** GJR-GARCH, the other standard asymmetric specification.
- **Engle, R. F. & Ng, V. K. (1993), "Measuring and Testing the Impact of News on Volatility",
  *Journal of Finance* 48(5), 1749-1778.** The news-impact curve.
