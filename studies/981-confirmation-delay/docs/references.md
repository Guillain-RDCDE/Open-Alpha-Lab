# Sources & literature map — Study 981 (The Price of Waiting)

## The rule, as practitioners state it

- **Wilder, J. W. (1978), *New Concepts in Technical Trading Systems*.** The source of RSI, and
  of much of the folklore about waiting for a signal to be "confirmed" before acting.
- **Murphy, J. J. (1999), *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*, ch. 9.** The textbook
  statement of confirmation and filters, including the percentage and time filters this study
  formalises as `k` consecutive sessions.
- **Kaufman, P. J. (2013), *Trading Systems and Methods*, 5th ed., ch. 8.** The most careful
  practitioner treatment of entry delays and whipsaw filters, with the trade-off stated
  explicitly.

## What the literature says about trend rules

- **Brock, W., Lakonishok, J. & LeBaron (1992), "Simple Technical Trading Rules and the
  Stochastic Properties of Stock Returns", *Journal of Finance* 47(5), 1731-1764.** The
  best-known moving-average study — and it includes a filter/band variant, the ancestor of the
  confirmation rule tested here.
- **Sullivan, R., Timmermann, A. & White, H. (1999), "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule
  Performance, and the Bootstrap", *Journal of Finance* 54(5), 1647-1691.** The reason this
  study reports the *distribution* of winning `k` rather than the best one.
- **Faber, M. (2007), "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation", *Journal of
  Wealth Management* 9(4), 69-79.** The 10-month moving-average rule; deliberately slow, which
  is one way of buying the same whipsaw reduction.
- **Zakamulin, V. (2014), "The Real-Life Performance of Market Timing with Moving Average and
  Time-Series Momentum Rules", *Journal of Asset Management* 15(4), 261-278.** Shows how much
  of the published advantage of such rules disappears out of sample — the backdrop to this
  study's first-half/second-half test.

## Whipsaw and transaction costs

- **Han, Y., Zhou, G. & Zhu, Y. (2016), "A Trend Factor: Any Economic Gains from Using
  Information over Investment Horizons?", *Journal of Financial Economics* 122(2), 352-375.**
  Combining horizons instead of delaying one.
- **Novy-Marx, R. & Velikov, M. (2016), "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 104-147.** Turnover is the tax on every timing rule;
  confirmation is one of the few free ways to cut it.
