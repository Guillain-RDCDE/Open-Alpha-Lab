# Sources & literature map — Study 985 (The Last Hike)

## The policy record itself

- **Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, "Open Market Operations" archive and the
  H.15 Selected Interest Rates release.** The target-rate changes hard-coded in
  `strategy.FOMC_MOVES`. Public, dated, never revised — which is why this study does not fetch
  them from a vendor.
- **Thornton, D. L. (2006), "When Did the FOMC Begin Announcing Its Policy Target?", Federal
  Reserve Bank of St. Louis *Review* 88(6).** Why February 1994 is the right start date: before
  it, "the date of the last hike" is an inference, not a record.

## Monetary policy and asset prices

- **Bernanke, B. S. & Kuttner, K. N. (2005), "What Explains the Stock Market's Reaction to
  Federal Reserve Policy?", *Journal of Finance* 60(3), 1221-1257.** The decomposition of the
  equity response into the expected and unexpected components of policy.
- **Kuttner, K. N. (2001), "Monetary Policy Surprises and Interest Rates", *Journal of Monetary
  Economics* 47(3), 523-544.** Why only the *surprise* component of a policy move should matter
  — and therefore why a cycle's ending, once anticipated, should not.
- **Rigobon, R. & Sack, B. (2004), "The Impact of Monetary Policy on Asset Prices", *Journal of
  Monetary Economics* 51(8), 1553-1575.** Identification through heteroskedasticity, and how
  small the measured effects are once endogeneity is handled.
- **Cieslak, A., Morse, A. & Vissing-Jorgensen, A. (2019), "Stock Returns over the FOMC Cycle",
  *Journal of Finance* 74(5), 2201-2248.** The even-week pattern — a nearby result that survives
  much better than this one, and a useful contrast.

## Real time versus hindsight

- **Orphanides, A. (2001), "Monetary Policy Rules Based on Real-Time Data", *American Economic
  Review* 91(4), 964-985.** The founding demonstration that policy analysis using revised data
  reaches conclusions unavailable to anyone at the time — the same error this study measures on
  the market side.
- **Diebold, F. X. & Rudebusch, G. D. (1991), "Forecasting Output with the Composite Leading
  Index: A Real-Time Analysis", *JASA* 86(415), 603-610.** A leading indicator that works in
  hindsight and not in real time, quantified.
- **Bry, G. & Boschan, C. (1971), *Cyclical Analysis of Time Series*, NBER.** The turning-point
  dating problem, and why a turning point cannot be identified until well after it happens.
