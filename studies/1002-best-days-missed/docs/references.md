# Sources & literature map — Study 1002 (The Ten Best Days)

## The statistic and its critics

- **Estrada, J. (2008), "Black Swans and Market Timing: How Not to Generate Alpha", *Journal of
  Investing* 17(3), 20-34.** Runs both halves across fifteen markets and finds the worst-days
  effect the larger — the direct antecedent of section 1 here.
- **Estrada, J. (2009), "Black Swans, Market Timing and the Dow", *Applied Economics Letters*
  16(11), 1117-1121.** The same exercise on 108 years of the Dow.
- **Kritzman, M. (2015), "What Practitioners Need to Know About Time Diversification", *Financial
  Analysts Journal* 71(1), 29-34.** On the misuse of compounding arithmetic in advice.

## Why the extreme days cluster

- **Mandelbrot, B. (1963), "The Variation of Certain Speculative Prices", *Journal of Business*
  36(4), 394-419.** "Large changes tend to be followed by large changes" — the original
  statement of the clustering that section 3 measures.
- **Engle, R. F. (1982), "Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity with Estimates of the
  Variance of United Kingdom Inflation", *Econometrica* 50(4), 987-1007.**
- **Cont, R. (2001), "Empirical Properties of Asset Returns: Stylized Facts and Statistical
  Issues", *Quantitative Finance* 1(2), 223-236.** The canonical list; volatility clustering and
  fat tails as separate facts, which is exactly the separation the synthetic control exploits.

## What accuracy a timer needs

- **Sharpe, W. F. (1975), "Likely Gains from Market Timing", *Financial Analysts Journal* 31(2),
  60-69.** The original break-even calculation: roughly 74% accuracy needed. Section 6 is a
  modern re-run under a different parameterisation.
- **Merriman, P. and others on the "missing the best days" genre**; see Estrada above for the
  academic treatment.
- **Chua, J. H., Woodward, R. S. & To, E. C. (1987), "Potential Gains from Stock Market Timing in
  Canada", *Financial Analysts Journal* 43(5), 50-56.** Separates bull-call and bear-call
  accuracy, which is the finer version of the hit rate used here.
