# Sources & literature map — Study 987 (Gold's Loud Cousin)

## The two metals

- **Escribano, A. & Granger, C. W. J. (1998), "Investigating the Relationship Between Gold and
  Silver Prices", *Journal of Forecasting* 17(2), 81-107.** The cointegration study: the two
  metals share a long-run relationship that broke down after the Hunt brothers episode. Directly
  relevant to whether a single beta can describe them.
- **Batten, J. A., Ciner, C. & Lucey, B. M. (2010), "The Macroeconomic Determinants of Volatility
  in Precious Metals Markets", *Resources Policy* 35(2), 65-71.** Gold and silver respond to
  *different* macro variables — the finding this study's residual section is designed to
  reproduce or refute.
- **Lucey, B. M. & Li, S. (2015), "What Precious Metals Act as Safe Havens, and When?", *Applied
  Economics Letters* 22(1), 35-45.** The regime dependence that makes a full-sample beta
  suspect.

## Industrial demand — silver's second driver

- **Ciner, C. (2001), "On the Long Run Relationship Between Gold and Silver Prices", *Global
  Finance Journal* 12(2), 299-303.** Argues the historical link has weakened as silver's
  industrial uses grew.
- **The Silver Institute, *World Silver Survey* (annual).** The demand breakdown — industrial
  versus investment versus jewellery — behind the claim that roughly half of silver demand is
  industrial. Photovoltaic demand in particular grew from negligible to a fifth of the total
  across this study's sample.

## Leverage arithmetic

- **Cheng, M. & Madhavan, A. (2009), "The Dynamics of Leveraged and Inverse Exchange-Traded
  Funds", *Journal of Investment Management* 7(4), 43-62.** The closed form for the compounding
  cost of a constant-leverage position: `β(β−1)σ²/2`, the term in `leverage_drag`.
- **Avellaneda, M. & Zhang, S. (2010), "Path-Dependence of Leveraged ETF Returns", *SIAM Journal
  on Financial Mathematics* 1(1), 586-603.** The exact path-dependence result behind the same
  approximation.

## Estimating and testing betas

- **Fama, E. F. & MacBeth, J. D. (1973), *JPE* 81(3), 607-636**, and **White, H. (1980),
  *Econometrica* 48(4), 817-838.** The heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors used
  throughout.
- **Ang, A. & Chen, J. (2002), "Asymmetric Correlations of Equity Portfolios", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 63(3), 443-494.** The up-market/down-market beta split in section 2, and
  why it usually finds something.
