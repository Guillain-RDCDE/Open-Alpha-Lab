# Sources & literature map — Study 1004 (How Many Stocks)

## The textbook result

- **Evans, J. L. & Archer, S. H. (1968), "Diversification and the Reduction of Dispersion: An
  Empirical Analysis", *Journal of Finance* 23(5), 761-767.** The original curve. Section 1
  reproduces it.
- **Statman, M. (1987), "How Many Stocks Make a Diversified Portfolio?", *Journal of Financial
  and Quantitative Analysis* 22(3), 353-363.** Argues for thirty on a cost-benefit basis — the
  first serious re-examination, and still on the volatility statistic.
- **Elton, E. J. & Gruber, M. J. (1977), "Risk Reduction and Portfolio Size: An Analytical
  Solution", *Journal of Business* 50(4), 415-437.** The closed form behind the curve's shape.

## Why the statistic is the wrong one

- **Bessembinder, H. (2018), "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?", *Journal of Financial
  Economics* 129(3), 440-457.** The skew result that drives section 4: most stocks
  underperform bills and a tiny minority account for all net wealth creation.
- **Bessembinder, H., Chen, T.-F., Choi, G. & Wei, K. C. J. (2019), "Do Global Stocks Outperform
  US Treasury Bills?"**, SSRN 3415739. The same finding out of sample, internationally.
- **Ikenberry, D., Shockley, R. & Womack, K. (1998), "Why Active Fund Managers Often
  Underperform the S&P 500: The Impact of Size and Skewness", *Journal of Private Portfolio
  Management* 1(1), 13-26.** Skewness making the median manager lag the mean — the same
  arithmetic as section 4, applied to managers rather than portfolios.
- **Simkowitz, M. A. & Beedles, W. L. (1978), "Diversification in a Three-Moment World",
  *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 13(5), 927-941.** Diversification destroys
  desirable skewness; an early statement that the variance criterion is incomplete.
- **Domian, D. L., Louton, D. A. & Racine, M. D. (2007), "Diversification in Portfolios of
  Individual Stocks: 100 Stocks Are Not Enough", *Financial Review* 42(4), 557-570.** Reaches a
  similar conclusion from shortfall risk rather than terminal-wealth dispersion.

## Method

- **Fisher, L. & Lorie, J. H. (1970), "Some Studies of Variability of Returns on Investments in
  Common Stocks", *Journal of Business* 43(2), 99-134.** The random-portfolio sampling design
  used throughout.
