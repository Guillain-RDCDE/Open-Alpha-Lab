# Sources & literature map — Study 972 (Adjusted or Not)

## The convention itself

- **CRSP, *Data Definitions and Calculations*.** The reference definition of a total-return
  series (distributions reinvested at the ex-date close) that every provider claims to follow.
- **Ince, O. S. & Porter, R. B. (2006), "Individual Equity Return Data from Thomson Datastream:
  Handle with Care!", *Journal of Financial Research* 29(4), 463-479.** The standard warning
  that return data must be checked before use; adjustment convention is the first thing to
  check.
- **Bali, T. G., Engle, R. F. & Murray, S. (2016), *Empirical Asset Pricing*, ch. 2-3.** How
  adjustment factors are built and where they go wrong.

## Why it matters for the cross-section

- **Jegadeesh, N. & Titman, S. (1993), "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers",
  *Journal of Finance* 48(1), 65-91.** The 12-1 momentum sort this study runs twice; the
  original is computed on total returns, and a price-only replication is a different strategy.
- **Novy-Marx, R. & Velikov, M. (2016), "A Taxonomy of Anomalies and Their Trading Costs",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 104-147.** How implementation choices — of which the
  return definition is the first — change measured anomaly performance.
- **Blitz, D., Huij, J. & Martens, M. (2011), "Residual Momentum", *Journal of Empirical
  Finance* 18(3), 506-521.** An example of how sensitive momentum rankings are to what exactly
  is being ranked.

## Dividends as a return component

- **Dimson, E., Marsh, P. & Staunton, M. (2002), *Triumph of the Optimists*, and the annual
  *Global Investment Returns Yearbook*.** The long-run arithmetic: over a century, reinvested
  dividends are the majority of the real total return of most equity markets. This study is
  that fact, measured on a modern fund universe.
- **Arnott, R. D. & Asness, C. S. (2003), "Surprise! Higher Dividends = Higher Earnings
  Growth", *Financial Analysts Journal* 59(1), 70-87.** Why yield is not a neutral
  characteristic to tilt away from by accident.
- **Elton, E. J. & Gruber, M. J. (1970), "Marginal Stockholder Tax Rates and the Clientele
  Effect", *Review of Economics and Statistics* 52(1), 68-74.** The tax wedge that makes the
  honest answer for a taxable holder sit *between* the two conventions.
