## Verdict

| Axis | Stamp | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Signal — is the effect real?** | ![Weak](https://img.shields.io/badge/Weak-dab617?style=flat-square) | The tested "fade" is **not real** — it is confidently *wrong-signed*: NVDA drifts **up** +4.76%/2wk (*t* = +2.13, placebo far-right p ≈ 0.985, jackknife-stable [+1.84, +2.46]) after the cluster, not down. What is genuinely present is an *upward* post-cluster drift — but it is textbook PEAD / recommendation drift measured on a single hand-picked mega-winner whose *random* windows already beat SPY by +2%, so it is neither novel nor the effect the folklore claims. Real-but-confounded and opposite-signed, not a real fade. |
| **Tradability — survives costs/scale?** | ![Mirage](https://img.shields.io/badge/Mirage-c0392b?style=flat-square) | Nothing to bank *as a fade* — the fade loses ~+4.7%/event before you even pay to short one of the most expensive-to-borrow, most-crowded names on the tape. The mirror long-the-drift survives gross+net but is one-name PEAD on top of a +2% selection baseline: no capacity story, no cross-sectional edge, not a strategy. |

> **In one sentence:** the "fade the analyst-upgrade cluster" trade is busted twice over on
> NVDA — the stock *drifts up* after the cluster (+4.8%/2wk, *t* = +2.1), so fading it loses
> money, and the only real effect in the data is a hand-picked, single-name post-earnings drift
> that is neither the claim nor a generalizable edge.
