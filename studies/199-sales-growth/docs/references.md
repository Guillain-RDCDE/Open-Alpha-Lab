# References — Study 199 (Sales-Growth)

## Primary paper

**Lakonishok, J., Shleifer, A., & Vishny, R. W. (1994).** Contrarian investment,
extrapolation, and risk. *Journal of Finance*, 49(5), 1541-1578.
[doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04772.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04772.x)

The foundational paper: LSV sort stocks into "glamour" (high past sales growth,
earnings growth, cash-flow growth) and "value" (low growth on the same metrics).
On all NYSE/AMEX stocks 1968-1990, the value quintile outperforms the glamour quintile
by roughly 10-11%/yr. They attribute this to investor over-extrapolation of past growth
and argue it is not explained by greater risk (beta, size, or standard deviation).

## Closely related papers

**Fama, E. F., & French, K. R. (1992).** The cross-section of expected stock returns.
*Journal of Finance*, 47(2), 427-465.
[doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04398.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1992.tb04398.x)

Documents the value premium using book-to-market ratio -- a related but distinct glamour
proxy. The book-to-market effect survives as the HML factor in the Fama-French three-factor
model. Sales-growth-based glamour/value is an alternative operationalisation that does not
require book values.

**La Porta, R., Lakonishok, J., Shleifer, A., & Vishny, R. W. (1997).** Good news for
value stocks: further evidence on market efficiency. *Journal of Finance*, 52(2), 859-874.
[doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb04825.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb04825.x)

Confirms the LSV results and shows that the value premium is driven by earnings surprises
-- value stocks systematically outperform expectations while glamour stocks disappoint.
This causal mechanism (over-extrapolation of growth) is more direct evidence for investor
behavioural error than pure risk premia.

**Doukas, J. A., Kim, C., & Pantzalis, C. (2002).** A test of the errors-in-expectations
explanation of the value/glamour stock returns performance: evidence from analysts'
forecasts. *Journal of Finance*, 57(5), 2143-2165.
[doi:10.1111/1540-6261.00491](https://doi.org/10.1111/1540-6261.00491)

Uses analyst forecast errors to confirm that glamour stocks are followed by negative
earnings surprises relative to expectation -- consistent with the over-extrapolation
story rather than rational risk premia.

**Cooper, M. J., Gulen, H., & Schill, M. J. (2008).** Asset growth and the cross-section
of stock returns. *Journal of Finance*, 63(4), 1609-1651.
[doi:10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01370.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01370.x)

A related over-investment/growth anomaly: firms with high total-asset growth earn lower
future returns. Distinct mechanism from revenue-growth extrapolation (this is about
capital allocation, not sales momentum). Tested in Study 44 (Growth-Spurt).

## Factor decay and replication literature

**McLean, R. D., & Pontiff, J. (2016).** Does academic research destroy stock return
predictability? *Journal of Finance*, 71(1), 5-32.
[doi:10.1111/jofi.12365](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12365)

Documents that anomaly returns decline significantly after publication. The LSV (1994)
glamour/value effect would be a prime candidate for post-publication decay given its
widespread citation and implementation by practitioners.

**Chordia, T., Subrahmanyam, A., & Tong, Q. (2014).** Have capital market anomalies
attenuated in the recent era of high liquidity and trading activity? *Journal of Accounting
and Economics*, 58(1), 41-58.
[doi:10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001)

Finds that many anomalies, including value-glamour effects, have weakened significantly
in the modern era of high liquidity and algorithmic trading.
