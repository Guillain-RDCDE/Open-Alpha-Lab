# References & literature map — Study 83 (Half-Life)

## The claim under test

- **The halving cycle narrative.** Bitcoin halvings (every ~210,000 blocks, roughly 4 years)
  cut the block reward in half, reducing new BTC supply by 50%. The folk theory, widely
  circulated by crypto-media and retail analysts (e.g. PlanB's Stock-to-Flow model, 2019),
  asserts: *"The supply shock from the halving predictably drives BTC price higher over the
  12–18 months that follow. Every halving has produced a new all-time high. This is a
  tradeable cycle, not luck."* We steelman this as: the post-halving 365-day forward return
  is systematically higher than the unconditional BTC drift, net of the secular bull market,
  and is predictable enough to act on.

## Why the steelman is *almost* coherent — the real effects it leans on

- **Supply shock economics.** Standard commodity supply-demand: halving the production rate of
  a fixed-cap asset, holding demand constant, should raise the equilibrium price. This is the
  theoretically sound core of the halving narrative. See: Mises (1912), *The Theory of Money
  and Credit* for the broader commodity-money framing; and Athey, Parashkevov, Sarukkai & Xia
  (2016), *Bitcoin Pricing, Adoption, and Usage: Theory and Evidence* (Stanford GSB Working
  Paper) for a modern crypto demand model.
- **Stock-to-Flow model (PlanB, 2019).** "Modelling Bitcoin Value with Scarcity" — a
  log-linear regression of BTC market cap on the stock-to-flow ratio. Predicts new ATHs
  post-halving. Widely cited but also widely criticised for in-sample fit with a structural
  break post-2022 (Coin Metrics, 2022). The model's predictions failed materially for the
  2024 cycle.
- **Anticipated vs unanticipated supply shocks.** In efficient markets, a *known* supply change
  (halvings are scheduled to the block) should be priced in *before* the event, not after. See:
  Fama (1970), *Efficient Capital Markets* (Journal of Finance). If markets are even weakly
  efficient, the halving is already in the price long before block 840,000 is mined.
- **"Buy the rumour, sell the news."** The 2024 halving is the cleanest test of the
  anticipation hypothesis: with 12 years of halving history, rational investors had every
  reason to pre-buy. The 2024 post-halving 365-day return was +35% (vs +265% in 2016 and
  +471% in 2020). Our secular-excess calculation shows the 2024 window *underperformed* BTC's
  own long-run trend — consistent with the supply shock being fully priced in.

## Why the claim fails the inference bar

- **The overfitting problem: n=4 is not a law.** With 4 lifetime halvings, a researcher
  cannot distinguish a genuine causal pattern from a coincidence driven by the 2010s–early
  2020s crypto secular bull market. Required reading: Lo & MacKinlay (1990), *Data Snooping
  Biases in Tests of Financial Asset Pricing Models* (Review of Financial Studies) — the
  formal treatment of why back-fitting a 4-point pattern to a noisy series is a mirage.
- **HAC t-stat with n=3.** Newey & West (1987), *A Simple, Positive Semi-Definite,
  Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent Covariance Matrix* (Econometrica) — our
  implementation in [`strategy.summarize`](../half_life/strategy.py). With n=3 and BTC's
  ~80% annual vol, a t-stat of +4 means nothing: the sample is too small and the underlying
  distribution too fat-tailed for standard asymptotics to apply.
- **The secular bull confounder.** Any 365-day BTC window over 2016–2021 produced enormous
  returns — not because of halvings, but because BTC's price rose ~10,000x over a decade.
  Our OLS secular-excess decomposition shows the 2016 and 2020 halvings occurred *during*
  the steepest part of the secular bull; the 2024 halving shows near-zero excess.
- **Random-day p-value: 0.112.** The 365-day halving mean (+1.111 log-ret) is *not* in the
  top 5% of random 3-window bundles drawn from the same tape. This is the key number: on
  the real BTC tape, many random 3-day starting points produce equally impressive 1-year runs.

## Method lineage

- **HAC / Newey-West t-stat.** Newey & West (1987), as above. [`strategy.summarize`](../half_life/strategy.py).
- **Monte Carlo p-value.** The random-control methodology follows the 'reality check' spirit
  of White (2000), *A Reality Check for Data Snooping* (Econometrica): compare the observed
  statistic against the empirical distribution generated by re-sampling from the same tape.
- **OLS secular-excess decomposition.** A simple detrending approach: fit log(price) ~ time,
  compute the predicted return for the halving window's calendar span, and subtract it. This
  is the 'abnormal return' concept from event-study methodology: Brown & Warner (1985),
  *Using Daily Stock Returns: The Case of Event Studies* (Journal of Financial Economics).
- **Reproducibility stamp.** Content fingerprint via SHA-1 on the close array; as-of date
  frozen in [`docs/results.md`](results.md).
