# References -- Study 197 (Dividend-Payout-Ratio)

## Primary source

**Arnott, R. D., & Asness, C. S. (2003).** Surprise! Higher dividends = higher
earnings growth. *Financial Analysts Journal*, 59(5), 70-87.
[DOI:10.2469/faj.v59.n5.2554](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v59.n5.2554)
*The original claim: at the aggregate S&P 500 level, a high dividend payout
ratio predicts higher subsequent real earnings growth. Tested here on the
Shiller monthly dataset 1882-2013.*

## Data sources

**Shiller, R. J. (1989, updated monthly).** Market Volatility. MIT Press.
Dataset available at: http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/data.htm
*Monthly S&P 500 data including price, dividends, earnings, CPI, and real
earnings / real price series from 1871 to present. Used here for the payout
ratio (Dividend / Earnings) and forward 10-year real outcomes.*

## Statistical methods

**Newey, W. K., & West, K. D. (1987).** A simple, positive semi-definite,
heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix.
*Econometrica*, 55(3), 703-708.
*The HAC estimator used with 120 lags to handle the severe overlap in
10-year forward regression windows.*

**Hodrick, R. J. (1992).** Dividend yields and expected stock returns:
alternative procedures for inference and measurement. *Review of Financial
Studies*, 5(3), 357-386.
*Classic reference on long-horizon regression inference and the importance
of correcting for overlapping windows.*

**White, H. (1980).** A heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance matrix
estimator and a direct test for heteroskedasticity. *Econometrica*, 48(4),
817-838.
*HC3 variant used for the non-overlapping annual robustness check.*

## Related studies and context

**Gordon, M. J. (1962).** The Investment, Financing, and Valuation of the
Corporation. Irwin.
*The Gordon Growth Model implies high payout ratio = low growth: Arnott-Asness
empirically challenges this at the aggregate level.*

**Miller, M. H., & Modigliani, F. (1961).** Dividend policy, growth, and the
valuation of shares. *Journal of Business*, 34(4), 411-433.
*Dividend irrelevance in a frictionless market: dividends should not matter
for valuation or growth. The Arnott-Asness finding challenges the frictionless
assumption at the macro level.*

**Fama, E. F., & French, K. R. (1988).** Dividend yields and expected stock
returns. *Journal of Financial Economics*, 22(1), 3-25.
*Dividend yield as a return predictor at aggregate level; related but distinct
from payout-ratio effects.*

**McLean, R. D., & Pontiff, J. (2016).** Does academic research destroy stock
return predictability? *Journal of Finance*, 71(1), 5-32.
*Post-publication decay framework: the OOS EG slope in this study does not
decay -- it strengthens, an unusual finding.*

**Zhou, P., & Ruland, W. (2006).** Dividend payout and future earnings growth.
*Financial Analysts Journal*, 62(3), 58-69.
*Cross-sectional study finding similar positive payout-EG relation at the
firm level for a subset of the S&P 500.*
