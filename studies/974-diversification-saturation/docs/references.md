# Sources & literature map — Study 974 (The Nth Asset)

## The curve itself

- **Markowitz, H. (1952), "Portfolio Selection", *Journal of Finance* 7(1), 77-91.** The origin
  of the idea that covariance, not variance, is what a portfolio is made of.
- **Evans, J. L. & Archer, S. H. (1968), "Diversification and the Reduction of Dispersion",
  *Journal of Finance* 23(5), 761-767.** The first empirical diversification curve (on stocks);
  the direct methodological ancestor of the random-subset approach used here.
- **Elton, E. J. & Gruber, M. J. (1977), "Risk Reduction and Portfolio Size: An Analytical
  Solution", *Journal of Business* 50(4), 415-437.** The closed form `σ²/k + ρσ²(k−1)/k` this
  study checks the empirical curve against.
- **Statman, M. (1987), "How Many Stocks Make a Diversified Portfolio?", *Journal of Financial
  and Quantitative Analysis* 22(3), 353-363.** The classic "how many" answer, and the first to
  make the stopping point an explicit cost-benefit decision rather than a number.

## Counting bets rather than assets

- **Meucci, A. (2009), "Managing Diversification", *Risk* 22(5), 74-79.** The effective number
  of bets: the entropy of principal-component variance contributions. Reported alongside the
  nominal count throughout this study.
- **Choueifaty, Y. & Coignard, Y. (2008), "Toward Maximum Diversification", *Journal of
  Portfolio Management* 35(1), 40-51.** The diversification ratio, the other standard way to
  say the same thing (and this desk's study **977**).
- **Bhansali, V., Davis, J., Rennison, G., Hsu, J. & Li, F. (2012), "The Risk in Risk Parity",
  *Journal of Investing* 21(3), 102-110.** Why nominal asset counts mislead when the exposures
  overlap.

## Where diversification fails

- **Ang, A. & Chen, J. (2002), "Asymmetric Correlations of Equity Portfolios", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 63(3), 443-494.** Correlations rise in downside markets, so the curve
  measured on the full sample overstates the protection available in a crisis.
- **Longin, F. & Solnik, B. (2001), "Extreme Correlation of International Equity Markets",
  *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 649-676.** The same result across countries.
- **DeMiguel, V., Garlappi, L. & Uppal, R. (2009), "Optimal Versus Naive Diversification",
  *Review of Financial Studies* 22(5), 1915-1953.** Why equal weight is the right benchmark for
  a study like this one.
