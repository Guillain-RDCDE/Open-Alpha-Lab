## Verdict

| Axis | Stamp | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Signal** — is the Brent–WTI spread tethered, and does fading it pay? | ![Weak](https://img.shields.io/badge/Weak-dab617?style=flat-square) | Tethered *within* an era, not across them. Over all 393 months the log Brent/WTI ratio is **not stationary** (ADF p = 0.45, KPSS rejects). A data-driven sup-F scan finds a mean break at **2010-09** (bootstrap p = 0.002 against a persistent, break-free AR(1)) that moved the dollar spread's average from **−1.41 to +7.69 $/bbl**. Before it, the ratio reverted with a **2.7-month half-life**. The real-time fader's gross HAC *t* is **+1.12**, short of 2. The pre-registered "Mixed" branch misses narrowly: the pre-break ADF p is 0.066 in logs, though below 0.001 in dollars. |
| **Tradability** — does it survive costs, the roll and the break? | ![Mirage](https://img.shields.io/badge/Mirage-c0392b?style=flat-square) | Net of costs the fader makes **+0.94%/yr (Sharpe +0.12, *t* +0.84)**, and that is on spot monthly averages nobody can trade, so it is an **upper bound** before the futures roll. The trader who calibrated on 1987–2009 went short the spread in 2010-05 and hit a **−24% maximum adverse excursion (−25 $/bbl)**. That trader was **still underwater 114 months later** when the tape ends. |

> **In one sentence:** Brent and WTI were tethered until a data-chosen break in 2010-09 moved the spread's home by about +9 $/bbl. Fading it in real time earned a gross *t* of 1.1 on untradeable spot prices, and the trader calibrated before 2010 spent 114 months underwater.

## What we tested

The claim at full strength: *Brent and WTI are the same light sweet crude in two places. The
spread is tethered by transport cost and always mean-reverts, so fade it when it stretches.*
That is the physical-arbitrage story behind every relative-value crude desk (see Fattouh 2010 in
the [references](docs/references.md)). We tested it on the frozen, SHA-pinned monthly Brent/WTI
spot tape shipped in `arch` (1987-05 → 2020-01). We ran ADF + KPSS and Engle–Granger on full and
sub-samples, and located the break by a bootstrapped sup-F, an HAC-CUSUM and Bai–Perron rather
than by hand. We estimated AR(1) half-lives on each side of the break, then booked a real-time
z-score fader (past data only, one lag, costs plus a roll toll) and the trader frozen on pre-2010
parameters. A synthetic OU spread with a `signal_strength` knob and an optional planted break
measures the tests' power at n = 393. ⚠️ **Spot prices are not investable**: a real trade
uses futures and pays the roll, so every P&L here is an upper bound.
**Dedup:** **05-twin-spread** and **23-broken-tether** test statistically selected *equity/ETF*
pairs, and the risk there is selection. **306-crack-spread** and **305-gold-oil-ratio** use a
commodity spread to *time equities*. Here the pair is chosen by physics rather than by search,
the anchor is infrastructure, and the question is what happens to a fader when that anchor moves.
