## Verdict

| Axis | Stamp | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Signal — is the effect real?** | ![Weak](https://img.shields.io/badge/Weak-dab617?style=flat-square) | The nominal stats cross the bar (*t* = −3.06, placebo two-tail p ≈ 0.04, jackknife-stable [−3.45, −2.23], consistent across cuts) — so it is **not** indistinguishable from noise. But it is a textbook **confound**: a static end-of-sample CEO snapshot turns the sort into a fixed short-tech/long-value sector bet (short leg = AAPL/AMZN/NVDA/TSLA/AMD), the monthly *t* overstates evidence for a fixed one-decade portfolio (real edge is one ~11-yr sector bet, Sharpe −0.9, driven by 2020 & 2026), and there is zero mechanism. Real number, wrong cause — hence Weak, not Real, and not None. |
| **Tradability — survives costs/scale?** | ![Mirage](https://img.shields.io/badge/Mirage-c0392b?style=flat-square) | Nothing to bank. The "edge" is an unrepeatable, look-ahead-contaminated sector coincidence dressed up as a name-length factor; it has no forward basis and would just be a levered ex-post bet against megacap tech. |

> **In one sentence:** sorting stocks by how long the CEO's surname is produces a
> significant-looking −0.9%/month long/short — but only because short surnames happened to
> belong to the megacap-tech winners (Cook, Jassy, Huang, Musk, Su), so the "signal" is a
> static-snapshot sector tilt with no mechanism: Weak, and a Mirage to trade.
