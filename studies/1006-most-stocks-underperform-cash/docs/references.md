# Sources & literature map — Study 1006 (Most Stocks Lose)

## The result

- **Bessembinder, H. (2018), "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?", *Journal of Financial
  Economics* 129(3), 440-457.** The paper. 57.4% of CRSP common stocks underperformed
  one-month bills over their lifetimes; the best 4% account for all net wealth creation.
- **Bessembinder, H., Chen, T.-F., Choi, G. & Wei, K. C. J. (2023), "Long-Term Shareholder
  Returns: Evidence from 64,000 Global Stocks", *Financial Analysts Journal* 79(3), 33-63.**
  The international replication, with a similar picture.
- **Heaton, J. B., Polson, N. G. & Witte, J. H. (2017), "Why Indexing Works", *Applied
  Stochastic Models in Business and Industry* 33(6), 690-693.** The clearest short statement of
  the reconciliation in section 4.
- **Ikenberry, D., Shockley, R. & Womack, K. (1998), "Why Active Fund Managers Often
  Underperform the S&P 500: The Impact of Size and Skewness", *Journal of Private Portfolio
  Management* 1(1), 13-26.** The same arithmetic applied to managers.

## Variance drag and the geometric mean

- **Fernholz, R. & Shay, B. (1982), "Stochastic Portfolio Theory and Stock Market Equilibrium",
  *Journal of Finance* 37(2), 615-624.** Rebalancing as a source of excess growth rate — the
  formal version of section 4.
- **Booth, D. G. & Fama, E. F. (1992), "Diversification Returns and Asset Contributions",
  *Financial Analysts Journal* 48(3), 26-32.** Decomposes the diversification return exactly as
  `index_reconciliation` does.
- **Willenbrock, S. (2011), "Diversification Return, Portfolio Rebalancing, and the Commodity
  Return Puzzle", *Financial Analysts Journal* 67(4), 42-49.** Clarifies that the diversification
  return is a rebalancing effect, not free money.
- **Latané, H. A. (1959), "Criteria for Choice Among Risky Ventures", *Journal of Political
  Economy* 67(2), 144-155.** The geometric-mean criterion.

## Skewness in the cross-section

- **Barberis, N. & Huang, M. (2008), "Stocks as Lotteries", *American Economic Review* 98(5),
  2066-2100.** Why investors might rationally accept the median outcome for a shot at the tail.
- **Bali, T. G., Cakici, N. & Whitelaw, R. F. (2011), "Maxing Out: Stocks as Lotteries and the
  Cross-Section of Expected Returns", *Journal of Financial Economics* 99(2), 427-446.**
