# Sources & literature map — Study 969 (Log or Simple)

## The arithmetic

- **Hudson, R. S. & Gregoriou, A. (2015), "Calculating and Comparing Security Returns Is
  Harder Than You Think: A Comparison Between Logarithmic and Simple Returns",
  *International Review of Financial Analysis* 38, 151-162.** The direct reference for this
  study: a careful catalogue of where the two conventions diverge and by how much.
- **Meucci, A. (2010), "Quant Nugget 2: Linear vs. Compounded Returns — Common Pitfalls in
  Portfolio Management", *GARP Risk Professional*, April 2010, 49-51.** The compact statement
  of the rule: compounded (log) returns aggregate across time, linear (simple) returns
  aggregate across assets.
- **Jensen, J. L. W. V. (1906), "Sur les fonctions convexes et les inégalités entre les valeurs
  moyennes", *Acta Mathematica* 30, 175-193.** The inequality that makes the portfolio error
  one-directional.

## Volatility drag and growth rates

- **Fernholz, R. & Shay, B. (1982), "Stochastic Portfolio Theory and Stock Market Equilibrium",
  *Journal of Finance* 37(2), 615-624.** The excess growth rate of a rebalanced portfolio — the
  quantity that the log-weighting mistake discards.
- **Kelly, J. L. (1956), "A New Interpretation of Information Rate", *Bell System Technical
  Journal* 35(4), 917-926**, and **Latané, H. A. (1959), "Criteria for Choice Among Risky
  Ventures", *Journal of Political Economy* 67(2), 144-155.** Why the geometric mean is the
  right objective for a compounding investor — and therefore why the convention is not merely
  cosmetic.
- **Booth, D. G. & Fama, E. F. (1992), "Diversification Returns and Asset Contributions",
  *Financial Analysts Journal* 48(3), 26-32.** Diversification return decomposed; the same
  σ²/2 term seen from the portfolio side.
- **Cheng, P. L. & Deets, M. K. (1971), "Portfolio Returns and the Random Walk Theory",
  *Journal of Finance* 26(1), 11-30.** An early demonstration that arithmetic averaging of
  returns overstates achievable performance.

## Where it bites in practice

- **Blitz, D. & van Vliet, P. (2007), "The Volatility Effect", *Journal of Portfolio
  Management* 34(1), 102-113.** Low-volatility portfolios look better on geometric returns than
  on arithmetic ones — an example of the convention changing a conclusion.
- **Roll, R. (1983), "On Computing Mean Returns and the Small Firm Premium", *Journal of
  Financial Economics* 12(3), 371-386.** The classic case where the rebalancing/aggregation
  convention created an apparent anomaly.
- **Willoughby, T. (2019 onward), practitioner notes on leveraged-ETF decay.** The
  best-known modern consequence, and this desk's studies 942 / 943 / 944.
