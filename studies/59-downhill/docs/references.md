# References & literature map — Study 59 (Downhill)

## The premium and its source

- **Fama, E., & Bliss, R. (1987).** *The Information in Long-Maturity Forward Rates.* American Economic
  Review — the term structure and the term premium.
- **Cochrane, J., & Piazzesi, M. (2005).** *Bond Risk Premia.* American Economic Review — predictability
  and time-variation of the term premium (why it can compress to ~zero).
- **Vendor / fixed-income family** — riding-the-yield-curve and carry strategies; backlog:
  [`docs/pwb_strategies_inventory.md`](../../../docs/pwb_strategies_inventory.md).

## On compression and the 2022 reversal

- **Kim, D., & Wright, J. (2005, Fed working paper)** — model-implied term premia have trended toward
  zero/negative in the QE era, consistent with our low Sharpe on duration.
- 2022 saw the worst Treasury drawdown in decades — the duration risk the premium is meant to pay for,
  realised.

## Data

- **Yahoo! Finance** — IEF (7–10y), SHY (1–3y), BIL (cash) iShares Treasury ETFs, monthly total return,
  2003–2026. The offline synthetic world gives long duration a real term premium *and* duration
  volatility (and a null), so "poorly-compensated risk" is provable offline.
